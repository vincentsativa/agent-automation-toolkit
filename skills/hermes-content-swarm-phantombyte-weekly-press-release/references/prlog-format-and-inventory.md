# PRLog.org Press Release Format and Inventory

## PRLog Free Submission Requirements

PRLog.org's free tier accepts press releases with three structural elements:

1. **Catchy title** (good SEO, under ~100 chars). Must be specific and value-driven. No "PhantomByte Weekly Update." Lead with the thing that shipped or the problem it solves. Never lead with a counter.
2. **Subtitle** (one line under the title). Pulls the reader in. Summarizes the "so what" in one sentence.
3. **Body** with the standard PRLog dateline format:

```
RONKONKOMA, N.Y. - [Month Day, Year] - PRLog -- [Opening paragraph: who, what, why it matters.]
```

Followed by 4 to 5 short body paragraphs, one quote from Vinny, one link to the thing being announced, and a one-sentence About line. No media-contact block.

## Structural Template (Rewritten October 14, 2026)

Measured off the Oct 7, 2026 Email Deliverability Rescue release, which is the reference standard for this format. One news hook, no boilerplate. Target 350 to 450 words. Hard cap 500. The 130-article, 156-article and 186-article releases all ran long, all reused the same skeleton, and are the reason this section was rewritten.

```
[Title: PhantomByte Launches/Announces <the specific thing> to <outcome>]

[Subtitle: one sentence. The problem it removes plus the mechanism. No adjectives about the company.]

RONKONKOMA, N.Y. - [Month D, YYYY] - PRLog -- [Paragraph 1: the announcement in one sentence, then the problem it addresses and why it matters now.]

[Paragraph 2: the specifics. How it works, in plain engineering terms.]

[Paragraph 3: what the buyer gets. Price, turnaround, deliverable. Concrete numbers.]

"[Quote from Vinny]" said Vinny Barreca, systems builder at PhantomByte. "[Second sentence in his voice. Direct, skeptical, no fluff.]"

[Paragraph 4: who it is for, named as concrete buyer types.]

[ONE link to the thing being announced.]

About PhantomByte: [ONE sentence. Name, location, what the studio builds.]

End
```

What is NOT in this template, deliberately, because it is what produced a year of identical releases: no production-numbers paragraph, no closing second quote, no five-property deep-link block, no formatted media-contact block. The Oct 7 release has none of them and is the strongest release in the newsroom.

## The AI Overview Parrot (Critical Insight, August 19, 2026)

Google's AI Overview does NOT form its own opinion of PhantomByte. It **parrots the press release nearly word-for-word**. Confirmed August 2026: searching "PhantomByte" returned an AI Overview that reproduced the exact sentences and structure from the PRLog release ("AI research and development studio based in Ronkonkoma, New York," "sovereign AI infrastructure," "publishing daily technical field notes"), citing the press releases as its sources.

**This means the monthly press release IS the control panel for Google's AI Overview and knowledge panel.** Whatever sentences go into the release come back as Google's authoritative description of the company. The release is reputation, not news.

Rules that follow:
- **Write sentences to be quoted.** The AI Overview mirrors your exact phrasing. Draft each identity line so that if Google repeats it word-for-word, you are happy with it. This is the strongest lever you have over what Google says about PhantomByte.
- **Repeat the same core identity lines, not the same release.** The overview echoes what it keeps seeing, so keep the short brand sentences identical across releases: what the studio builds, the Ronkonkoma base, sovereign AI infrastructure, systems builder Vinny Barreca. Everything else, the headline, the subtitle, the opening sentence, the body paragraphs, is written fresh for this month's hook. Repeating the whole document with one number changed is what broke four releases in a row.
- **Control every adjective and title.** Whatever descriptor goes in the release is what Google uses. There is no moderation layer correcting it. Never let a word you do not want in your permanent bio slip into a release.
- **Verify the overview after publishing.** After each release is live, check what the AI Overview says when you search "PhantomByte." If it picked up a wrong label, the next release must state the correct one plainly and repeatedly until the overview flips.

## SEO Strategy for PRLog (Critical Insight, July 14, 2026)

Google crawls PRLog press releases to learn what PhantomByte IS. The press release is not just a news announcement. It is a **brand-identity document for Google's index**. Treat it with the question: "What do we want Google to know about us?"

Key signals to embed in every PRLog release:
- **Five-property ecosystem**: the studio spans five properties (phantom-byte.com, phantom-byte.com/tutorials, articles.phantom-byte.com, vincentsativa.com, phantombyteai.gumroad.com). Describe the ecosystem in prose when it is relevant. Do not dump a five-link block into every release.
- **Sovereign AI / local-first positioning**: This is the brand's core differentiator. Every release should reinforce it.
- **Production scale numbers**: article count, site count, agent skills, daily automations. Include these only when they are the story. They are not a standing paragraph that goes into every release.
- **Open-source stack**: Ollama, Hermes Agent, OpenClaw, Pi Coding Agent. Google should associate these with PhantomByte.
- **Services with price points**: Concrete pricing signals that this is a real business, not a blog.
- **AI agent debugging as a category**: vincentsativa.com is a dedicated debugging hub. This is a distinct service category Google should index.
- **Operator name**: "Vinny Barreca" should appear in every release for author authority. NEVER call him "founder", he is a systems builder / operator. Use "Vinny Barreca" or "systems builder Vinny Barreca" or just "Vinny Barreca."

## What Counts as a News Hook (Added October 14, 2026)

A release goes out only when something shipped. The hook must be one of these:

- A product or service launch. This is the strongest hook in the set. The Oct 7, 2026 Email Deliverability Rescue release is the model.
- A new free resource, guide, tool, or checker.
- A shipped capability or architectural shift.
- A proprietary finding or benchmark.

An article count is NOT a news hook. It is a counter. It carried the May 8, Jul 14, Aug 14 and Sep 14 releases, and by August the releases had collapsed into the same document with a larger number in the title. Four releases in a row shared the same subtitle shape, the same production-numbers paragraph, the same five-link block, and the same About block. That is the failure this section exists to prevent.

Hard rules:

- Never lead with an article count two releases in a row.
- Never repeat the previous release's headline pattern, subtitle shape, or opening sentence.
- If the only thing that changed since the last release is a number going up, there is no hook. Say so and do not write a release.

## Workflow for Writing a New PRLog Release

1. **Check the newsroom**: Fetch https://pressroom.prlog.org/Phantom-Byte and the RSS feed at https://pressroom.prlog.org/Phantom-Byte/latest.xml with scrapling in the terminal to see every previous release and how recently one went out.
2. **Review brand websites**: Fetch all five properties with scrapling to understand current state and positioning. Capture the article count, tutorial count, service count, and any new property, and note whether anything actually shipped since the last release.
3. **Read recent articles**: Visit articles.phantom-byte.com and read at least one recent article to capture Vinny's voice for the quote.
4. **Identify the news hook**: name the one thing that shipped since the last release. Check it against "What Counts as a News Hook" above. If the only change is the article count going up, stop. There is no release this month.
5. **Write the release**: Follow the structural template above, which is modelled on the Oct 7, 2026 Email Deliverability Rescue release. Include a Vinny quote in his voice (direct, skeptical, no fluff). **Write every identity sentence to be quoted verbatim by Google's AI Overview** (see "The AI Overview Parrot" above): reuse the short stable brand lines from prior releases, and write everything else fresh for this month's hook.
6. **Write to file**: Save as `~/phantombyte_press_release_[date].txt` with proper extension.
7. **Email**: Send as attachment to hello@phantom-byte.com via the email-sender skill.
8. **Telegram notification**: Just "Email sent. Check your inbox for the press release." Nothing more. The email is the delivery mechanism.
9. **Verify the overview**: After the release is published, search "PhantomByte" and check the AI Overview. Note in the next release anything that needs correcting so Google flips it back.

## Pitfalls

- Do NOT repeat the same milestone as a previous release. Check the inventory table above before writing.
- Do NOT reuse the previous release's headline pattern, subtitle shape, or opening sentence. Diff the draft against the last release in the newsroom and rewrite anything that matches. This is the check that four consecutive releases failed.
- Do NOT put production numbers, a five-link block, or a media-contact block in the release. Those were boilerplate carried forward from the old template and they are what made every release the same document.
- Do NOT write a release at all when the only change since last month is a counter going up.
- Do NOT use marketing fluff. The tone is technical, authoritative, written for engineers. The phantom-byte-writing-style skill applies.
- Do NOT forget the dateline format: "RONKONKOMA, N.Y. - [Date] - PRLog --"
- Do NOT add a five-property deep-link block or a media-contact block. Link only the thing being announced, the way the Oct 7 release does.
- Do NOT write the press release content into the Telegram message. Email is the delivery. Telegram is the doorbell.
- Do NOT use em dashes anywhere in the release. Use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- ALWAYS include a quote from Vinny. It builds author authority for Google.
- ALWAYS verify every number that does appear in the release against the live website, not from memory.
