# Commit to ONE Verdict, No Flip-Flopping Across a Research Thread

## The failure (Aug 15, 2026)
Vinny asked "What happened to AI.com?" The agent gave THREE contradictory
verdicts in one conversation:
1. "It's a marketing play" (no real product intent)
2. "It's a failure" (dead shell)
3. "It's a real, working product" (not a failure)

Each verdict matched whatever source had just been read. Vinny's reaction:
"You talk out of all sides of your ass." A flip-flop is worse than a single
wrong answer because it destroys trust in every answer you give.

## Root cause
The agent treated each new source as a fresh verdict instead of weighing all
sources and committing. A viral narrative (one engineer's X post about a
"$500 engineering budget") was allowed to override the user's own direct
experience. Worse, the agent cited AI-generated content farms as "independent
reviews", they fabricated "I tested it" claims about a product that was
actually gated behind a waitlist.

## The rules
1. Do NOT revise your verdict to match each new source you read. Sources
   conflict; your job is to weigh them and commit, not echo the last one.
2. Reconcile, don't swap. If a later source contradicts your earlier answer,
   fold both into ONE position rather than replacing your verdict.
3. The user's direct experience is the ground truth. If Vinny says a product
   doesn't work, no "review" overrides him, especially not an AI-generated
   one. (See research-source-standards §4.5.)
4. NEVER cite AI-generated review sites or SEO content farms as evidence.
   A review is only evidence if a named human actually tested the product.
   Check for AI-drafting disclosures (e.g. "researched and drafted using AI
   tools") and named human authors before citing any review.
5. If you genuinely cannot determine the truth, say "I don't know" once and
   stop. Do not keep searching and keep changing your answer.
6. When you correct yourself, correct ONCE and hold the corrected position.
   Do not re-correct again later in the same thread, that is the flip-flop
   pattern.

## Worked example (AI.com, 2026), CORRECTED
- Domain: $70M purchase by Crypto.com CEO Kris Marszalek (April 2025), most
  expensive domain sale ever.
- Super Bowl LX (Feb 8, 2026): $8M ad, site crashed instantly under traffic.
- Product: consumer AI agent platform, an OpenClaw wrapper. BUT the user
  signed up after the Super Bowl and was still on a waitlist; no one on the
  internet had actually gotten an agent. The platform is NOT functioning for
  users, it is a landing page that collects handles and gates everyone
  behind a waitlist.
- The "independent reviews" (Agent Finder 7/10, AI Tool Discovery) that
  claimed it worked were AI-generated content farms. agent-finder.co
  disclosed "Atlas researched and drafted this article using AI-assisted
  tools." They fabricated their testing. Do NOT trust them.
- Correct single verdict: "AI.com is not functioning. It's a landing page
  that collects handles and puts everyone on a waitlist. The $70M domain,
  the $8M ad, the crash, and the 'AGI' claims were the product. The actual
  agent platform doesn't exist for users."
- The earlier "it works" verdict was WRONG, it came from trusting content
  farms over the user's real experience.
