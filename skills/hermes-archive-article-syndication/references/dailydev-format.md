# daily.dev Post Format

A daily.dev post is a link post with a title and a short body, not a republished article. There is no canonical tag and no full body to hand over. The post drives a click into the article it links.

## Shape

- **Title**: a clickable headline. Write it as a claim with a specific stakes, not a topic label. `Your Data Center Is About to Leave the Planet. The Power Grid Already Lost.` Not `Google orbital data center news`.
- **Body**: three to five short paragraphs that stand alone without the link. Lead with the news fact and the date or window, give the figures that make it concrete, name what the article predicted, then close with the link on its own line.
- **Link**: the full article URL at the end, on its own line. Plain URL, no anchor text.
- **Tags**: a short line of hashtags at the bottom. Five terms is the practical maximum; keep them topical and relevant to the article's subject rather than broad.

## Choosing which article to post

1. Fetch the profile and enumerate every existing post (see SKILL.md Step 6 for the fetch command and the permalink shape).
2. List the subjects already covered. Every one of them is off the table.
3. Pick the article whose subject is untouched by that list AND whose argument today's news just confirmed. A prediction that a fresh headline ratifies is the strongest possible post for a link-driven platform.
4. If a daily data file is in the task, pull the new facts from it and say which ones are new to the story.

## Pitfalls

- Do not post about an article whose subject is already covered by an existing post on the profile, even if the article itself is unpublished there. The subject is the unit of novelty, not the URL.
- Do not write the post as an article abstract. The post carries the new news fact and the link; the article carries the full argument.
- Do not deliver the post as chat text. Write it into the package file with the other platform versions.
