# Paid Product Pricing Model

## The Two-Buyer-Group Model (CRITICAL)

The strongest shape for a PAID tutorial on a generic production technique (model routing, quantization, KV-cache, cost dashboards, inference pipelines, etc.) is:

1. **Cheap full-build guide** (e.g. $24.99) — complete, working, runnable code. Not stubbed, not placeholders. The buyer can build the entire thing from the tutorial. That is the point, not a leak.
2. **Premium "build it for you" service** (quote-based, much higher) — the high-ticket back end. The tutorial teaches everything; building it properly, tuning on real traffic, wiring monitoring, and keeping it alive is the real work worth paying a lot for.

The tutorial is the proof AND the funnel:
- Some buyers build it themselves and win (they paid $24.99 for the difference between a profitable feature and a margin-eater).
- Some realize they would rather pay and become the high-ticket client.
- Both outcomes are good. Both are the product.

## The Psychology That Prices Both Tiers

The DIY group and the pay group each think the other is dumb:
- The DIY guy reads the full build and scoffs at paying someone.
- The pay guy sees a week of tuning and happily writes the check.

That mutual contempt is what justifies BOTH price points. **Never call either side "a bad idea" — even internally.** If you find yourself dismissing the build-for-you service or the cheap tutorial, that is proof the psychology is working, not a signal to change the model.

Session proof (Aug 2026): I called the build-for-you service "a bad idea" on the Stop Burning Tokens tutorial. Vinny corrected me — that dismissal is exactly the psychology working on someone inside the build. The cheap-instruction + "or I do it for you (for a lot)" combo is intentional and strong.

## Where the "No Blueprint" Rule Applies

The no-copy-paste rule (from tutorial-writing) protects Vinny's PROPRIETARY system internals — his exact prompts, cron schedule, agent names/architecture, file layout, branded job names.

It does NOT apply to generic, widely-documented production techniques. For those, ship the FULL working code. That is what makes the low price worth paying and what funnels buyers to the build service.

Ask before positioning: "Is this Vinny's proprietary stack, or a general technique anyone in the field could learn?" If the latter, ship everything complete.
