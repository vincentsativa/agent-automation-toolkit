# Title Formatting Rules for PhantomByte Articles

> Session-hardened rules from direct user corrections. **The default is: the outline's title is used verbatim, character for character.** Never shorten, reword, or substitute a title Vinny selected. The rules below apply ONLY when Vinny explicitly tells you to change that specific title.

## Rule 0: The Title Is Locked Until Vinny Unlocks It

Vinny picks the outline, so the title is his. It goes into every artifact exactly as written in the outline: the draft file, the "COMPLETE" rewrite, any revision pass, the email subject, the Pinterest title, and chat. A long title stays long. A subtitle after a colon stays in.

- Do NOT shorten it on your own, and do NOT do it silently.
- Do NOT treat a general "implement these revision suggestions" or "make a COMPLETE version" pass as permission to touch the title. It is not.
- If you think a different title reads better, say so in chat and change nothing in the file.
- Reason this rule exists: the old default of shortening long outline titles on sight shipped documents under titles Vinny never chose, and he only found out days later. That is the failure this rule prevents.

## Rule 0A: The Keyword Optimization Pass Runs AFTER the Article Is Written

Title keyword optimization is a post-writing step, never a pre-writing one. You cannot target keywords for an article that does not exist yet, and a title optimized from the outline alone is guessing at what the piece actually covers.

- **Order of operations:** read the outline, write the FULL article body first, then optimize the title against the finished article and its real keywords.
- **The optimized title is the one that ships:** line 1 of the article file, the email subject, and the Pinterest title all carry it.
- **Optimize, never trim.** Keyword optimization means naming what the article is about in the words a searcher would actually type. Keep the entity and proper nouns (OpenAI textGrain), keep the numbers, keep the concrete payoff. What you are fixing is a clever phrase that names nothing.
- **Reconcile with Rule 0 this way:** the outline title is the raw material, and the optimized title has to be MORE findable than it, never merely shorter or more generic. If the optimization is not clearly more searchable, keep the outline title as written.
- **Report the final title in the delivery message** so Vinny sees what shipped and can overrule it in one line.

## Rule 1: Never Use All Caps

**User instruction (July 4, 2026):** "Do NOT use all caps in the title, I hate that shit."

- Do NOT capitalize any entire word in the title (e.g., "NEEDS," "DEBUGGER," "LAYER")
- Use title case or sentence case only
- "Your Agent Is the Debugger" — correct
- "Your Agent IS the DEBUGGER" — incorrect, will be rejected

## Rule 2: Shorten Aggressively, But ONLY When Vinny Says "Too Long"

**User instruction (July 4, 2026):** "please shorten the title, without effecting impact. it's just too long"

**Scope: this rule fires only on an explicit instruction about that specific title.** "This is too long, shorten it" is the trigger. An outline with a long title is NOT a trigger — long titles ship as written. Absent that explicit instruction, see Rule 0.

When Vinny explicitly says the title is too long, do not ask how short he wants it. Shorten it aggressively yourself.

**Concrete shortening patterns:**

| Original | Shortened | Drop Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "The Data Center Target: Why Your GPU Cluster Is Now a Military Asset" | "Your GPU Cluster Is a Military Target Now" | Drop "The" prefix, drop colon clause, convert "Why X Is Y" to "X Is Y" |
| "The Skill Architecture Revolution: Why Your Next Agent Won't Be a Monolith" | "Your Agent Is a Monolith. That's the Problem." | Drop "The" prefix, drop subtitle clause, flip to punchy statement |
| "Your Agent Needs a Debugger: Why Tool-Operator Agents Are the New Infrastructure Layer / How ProxyBoy, Qpilot, and LockIn MCP Reveal the Next Phase of Agent Architecture" | "Your Agent Is the Debugger" | Drop everything after the first noun phrase that delivers the claim. 7 words vs 26. |

**Shortening checklist:**
1. Drop "The" and "Why" prefixes
2. Remove subtitle clauses after colons when the main clause carries the weight
3. Convert "Why X Is Y" constructions to "X Is Y"
4. Remove tool-name lists from the title (save them for the subtitle or body)
5. Keep the noun phrase that delivers the punch
6. Target under 10 words if possible

**Psychology:** Once Vinny has said a specific title is too long, he will tell you if you went too far. He will NOT tell you if you didn't go far enough. But this applies ONLY after he says it. Never shorten a title he has not flagged.

## Rule 3: Verify Before Sending

Verify after the title pass, whichever pass applied:

- **After the post-writing keyword optimization (Rule 0A):** confirm line 1 of the file carries the optimized title, that it contains the leading entity and every number the outline's title carried, and that it is at least as specific as the title it replaced. If it is vaguer or shorter-but-wider, revert to the outline title.
- **After a shortening Vinny explicitly ordered (Rule 2):** count the words. If it is still over 12 words, shorten again.
- In every case: check for all-caps words and em dashes.
- Report the title that shipped in the delivery message.
- Then, and only then, proceed to email or deliver.
