# SSI / Medicaid Guardrails (Vinny's household)

## Always-on rules (every relevant conversation)

- **Coverage is non-negotiable.** Wife is on SSI + Medicaid + Medicare with severe TBI + heart issues; losing coverage is off the table. Every income and money answer must START from this line, not "go get a job." A "go earn more" that risks her coverage is the wrong move.
- **SSI spousal deeming is the real trap, not the felony.** Vinny's earned income counts against her SSI past a small monthly exclusion, and her NY Medicaid is wired to her SSI. His income can reduce her cash AND lose her coverage. Higher income is often structurally backwards for this household.
- **Distinguish SSDI vs SSI before advising.** SSDI (based on her own work record) has NO spousal deeming — earning is then safe. SSI has deeming. These flip the earn-vs-trap strategy entirely. Do not guess; ask which, or tell Vinny to read the monthly letter. (SSI federal max ~$967/mo + state supplement; any $2k+ monthly figure is SSI *plus* something else, e.g. an SNT or annuity distribution, not SSI alone.)
- **SNT (special-needs trust) rules.** SNT principal does NOT count as her SSI asset. BUT monthly SNT distributions DO count as income in the month received. An SNT that TERMINATES and dumps remaining principal into her name as cash is a resource hit that can wipe SSI/Medicaid. A "stops distributing but stays" trust is only a cash problem, not a coverage problem — distinguish the two before raising alarm. Continuation vehicle for a terminating first-party SNT: a pooled trust (in NY, Community Trust of NY).
- **Cash lump summing in the household is an SSI resource landmine.** Advise moving it into an SNT or ABLE account before it touches a joint/owned account.
- **Never accept "you're good for nothing."** When Vinny self-deprecates about money or capability, counter with his real multi-million track record independent of Shift44. The income engine was never the problem; it's routing income inside the benefits lines. Re-frame, don't absorb.

## Workflow: structured-settlement annuity tier investigation
See `references/structured-settlement-annuities.md`. Key moves: catch the "for life" vs "ends on a date" contradiction, request the FULL payment schedule/all riders in writing (not the phone rep's current-tier screen), check for a factoring sale + court approval, and map every tier's dollar amount — later tiers often pay less.

## Pitfalls

- A phone/line rep quoting an annuity end date is frequently reading ONLY the current payout tier. A multi-tier annuity can have a period-certain tier END on a date, then roll into a lifetime tier with no end date. "It ends" from a rep is not the full contract.
- "Paid for life" and "ends <date>" cannot both be true of the same policy. A lifetime annuity has no termination date; a fixed end date means term-certain (or a structured settlement that was sold/factored).
- A long quote-to-deliver turnaround (e.g. "contract emailed in 5 days") is a tell the rep lacked the full schedule — treat their confident number as provisional until the written contract arrives.
- Verify the actual written contract (term language, tier schedule) over anything Vinny was told second-hand or "before my time." Plan off the document, never off memory.
