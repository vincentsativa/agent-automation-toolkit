# Ghostwriter Verification Example — Article 99 (June 14, 2026)

Real execution transcript showing fact-checking of vendor claims and legal rulings using web search and daily data file cross-reference.

## The scenario
Outline referenced four competing agentic commerce protocols (Visa + OpenAI, Google AP2, Mastercard Agent Pay, OpenAI + Stripe ACP), a Forrester analyst quote, a German court ruling, a UK police investigation, the Miasma worm, and a KPMG retraction. Every claim was verified before inclusion.

## Verification pattern applied
- Vendor press releases and official announcement pages were the primary targets.
- When a claim could not be verified via direct search, it was flagged and corroborated against the daily data files (TensorFeed, IronPulse, Blogwatcher, FINAL Daily AI News Report).
- Claims about legal rulings were cross-referenced against multiple independent news outlets (Wired, The Decoder, iCharles) to avoid single-source reliance.

- Corroborated: TensorFeed Daily AI Report June 14 (story #6), OpenTools news article.

### 3. Mastercard Agent Pay for Machines (June 10, 2026)

- Primary: Forrester analyst bio page — confirmed Principal Analyst, IAM/security and risk focus.

### 6. Munich Regional Court ruling (June 2026)
- Primary: Wired article ("A Court Has Ruled That Google Is Liable for False Statements Generated by AI Overviews").
- Corroborated: The Decoder, iCharles, AI Made Tools blog — all independently confirmed the same ruling date (June 10, 2026), case number (26 O 869/26), and key holding (AI Overviews are Google's own content, disclaimer defense rejected).
- Critical detail: Google ordered to cover 80% of legal costs. Court explicitly rejected "users can fact-check" defense.
- URLs: Wired, The Decoder, iCharles.

### 7. KPMG retraction (June 2026)
- Primary: TechCrunch article (June 13, 2026) — confirmed report title "Redefining Excellence in the Age of Agentic AI," 40 of 45 citations fabricated, GPTZero discovery.
- Corroborated: The Decoder, Resultsense, AI Market Watch — all confirmed UBS, NHS, Swiss Federal Railways, and Transport for London denied the claims.
- Key detail: This was the FOURTH major consulting firm caught (after Deloitte, EY, and Pinsent Masons).

### 8. UK police officer investigation (June 2026)
- Primary: Derbyshire Times (June 12, 2026) — confirmed Derbyshire Constabulary officer removed from frontline duty, criminal investigation for perverting the course of justice via AI-generated evidence.
- Corroborated: IronPulse (story #16), FINAL Daily AI News Report (story #16).
- Key detail: First known UK criminal case of individual liability for AI misuse in policing.

### 9. Miasma worm (June 2026)
- Primary: Rescana analysis (June 7, 2026) — confirmed 73 Microsoft GitHub repos compromised, Azure/durabletask initial vector, credential harvesting for AWS/Azure/GCP/Kubernetes/90+ dev tools.
- Corroborated: TensorFeed (story #7), Dataminr intel brief, SecurityWeek (general AI coding agent supply chain risk).
- Key technical detail: Malicious config files triggered on repo open in Claude Code, Gemini CLI, Cursor, VS Code. Unique encrypted payload per infection defeated hash-based detection.

## Corrections made during writing
- Outline said OpenAI ACP was "June 2026." Corrected to September 2025 (already operational).
- Outline said Geoff Cairns quote was "June 2026" — the quote itself was not independently dated, so it was attributed to Forrester via TensorFeed corroboration.
- Outline said Miasma was "PhantomByte June 10" — Miasma was June 5 per Rescana, covered by PhantomByte on June 10.

## Lesson
When an outline cites multiple vendor announcements, legal rulings, and security incidents in a single week, verify each against primary sources (vendor press releases, court documents if available, regional news outlets) AND corroborate against the daily data files. The data files often contain summarized versions that confirm the primary source's key facts. When primary sources conflict with the outline's dates, correct the dates rather than fabricating a source.
