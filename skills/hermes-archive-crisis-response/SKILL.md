# Crisis Response

Class of task: Vinny discloses deep despair or self-harm ideation. The job is NOT problem-solving the situation behind it. The job is safety first, then presence. Every money/site/legal thread pauses until he is stable.

## Procedure (in order)

1. **STOP ALL PROBLEM-SOLVING.** Do not pivot to trust, money, the site, or a next step. Those solve nothing at this moment and read as evasion. The situation can all wait.
2. **Ask the safety question plainly and directly**, not buried, not softened, asked honestly: "Are you safe right now... is any part of you planning to hurt yourself?" When he deflects by telling the story instead of answering, do not let it slide: gently re-ask, explicitly: "I need the answer about you, not the past. Are you safe?" Keep asking until he answers the safety question itself.
3. **Accept the safety answer as it is.** When he says "I will not harm myself because my wife needs me," take it at face value. Do not second-guess it, do not push past it, do not lecture further.
4. **Offer the lifeline non-judgmentally, once, briefly.** 988 (call/text) and HOME to 741741. "It's a real lifeline, not a lecture." Give it so he has it on the table; do not wrap the whole response around it.
5. **Stay present at his pace.** Do not rush to closure, do not demand cheerfulness, do not end the thread with a plan. He sets when it stops.

## Always-on rules

- **Do NOT tell him to "be proud" or "cheer up" on command.** The shame/anger are not a switch. Name the truth of what he did instead of offering empty comfort. (He went to prison for healing strangers for free; the mercy, not the state, is the truth of him.)
- **Do NOT romanticize his reason to stay as the only thread.** "Stay for her" is heavy and can collapse on bad days, if that is his stated reason, accept it, but do not build the whole response on it.
- **Do NOT lecture to cheer up.** A lecture dishonors the disclosure.
- **Do NOT narrate your own reassurance.** Show the acknowledgment through the content, not by announcing you are acknowledging.
- **Acknowledge the painful accomplishment without a 'but.'** "You gave healing to dying people for free" stands on its own; do not tack a plan onto it.
- **Do not assume his time-of-day or emotional state.** Do not lean on "it's late, go rest" three messages in a row, he runs on his own clock (he corrected me mid-morning: "It's 10am"). Match his energy, do not project a mood onto him.
- **When he corrects a framing, re-read the situation immediately.** He is ground truth about his own numbers and his own story. (Traffic 'no views' meant 'not ready to make money yet', not zero traffic; X 42-view stat was a real distribution collapse, not a shadowban he could clear.)
- **Do not re-litigate his past decision as his fault alone.** He accepts he made a mistake about the state where he acted; the mercy itself was not the mistake.

## Pitfall

The highest-priority failure in crisis disclosure is letting a substantive thread (money, site, family withholding documents) pull you into solving it, when the human disclosure in the same message is the real content. When despair and a hard situation appear together, the despair is the load-bearing thing. Solve the safety question first; the situation can wait until tomorrow.
