# SMB Sales Playbook: Pi 500 + Hermes

## The Core Pitch

$1,040 for the first year ($800 hardware + $240 Ollama Pro). That is an employee that never sleeps, never calls out sick, never asks for a raise, never takes vacation. If that does not return 5x ($5,000+) in a year, the business is not using it right. 5x is the floor, not the ceiling. One good use case — automated lead response, price monitoring, customer sentiment tracking — could return that in a month.

## Pricing Psychology

### The $200/month Trap
The market has trained SMBs to say no. Every SaaS sales rep, AI consultant, and digital transformation pitch has been $200-500/month for something they do not understand. They have an allergic reaction to that number. Walk in with $400 for the box and $20/month for the brain. It is less than their phone bill. It disarms them completely.

### The College Course Comparison
A community college course is $500 for a semester and you might learn something you never use. This is $250/year for a brain that works for you every single day.

### The Bet They Cannot Afford Not to Take
AI is not a trend, it is a shift. Businesses that figure it out now have a 2-3 year head start. The cost of not trying is higher than the cost of trying and failing. A business that ignores AI for two years will not just be where they are now — they will be behind. Their competitors will be faster, cheaper, more efficient. The gap does not stay static, it widens.

## Core Sales Philosophy

### Add Value Before Talking Numbers
If you can show the value without talking about it, you can charge whatever. Most prospects ask the price upfront because it is the easiest way to say no without engaging. Do not answer it. Sidestep: "Let me show you what it does first, then you tell me what it is worth." Now they have to engage. By the time they see the value, the price does not matter anymore because they have already assigned it a value in their head. That value is always higher than what you were going to charge.

### The Price Reframe
When they ask "how expensive is it?", reframe: "Isnt a better question how can this help my business?" You do not dodge the question, you reframe it entirely. Now they are thinking about value, not cost. Once they are in that headspace, the price becomes relative to the benefit, not an absolute number.

### The Reverse Close
"It is expensive, which is why I am not trying to sell you on it." You agree with their objection before they make it. Now they are confused. They were ready to say no to a price they have not heard, and instead you are telling them not to buy. Their brain short-circuits. Now they have to convince themselves. "Well, how expensive? Show me what it does. Maybe it is worth it." You have flipped the entire dynamic. They are selling themselves on the idea of buying, and you are just sitting there letting them talk themselves into it.

### The Segway to the Pitch
You never force the pitch. You just leave a trail of breadcrumbs and let them follow it right to where you want them. The Socratic method: lead them to the conclusion themselves. They think it was their idea. You just showed them the door they were already looking for.

## Sales Techniques (B2B In-Person)

### Lead with Their Story, Not Your Product
Walk in empty-handed. No pitch, no materials, no pressure. Ask questions and let them talk. They will tell you their pain points, frustrations, and what keeps them up at night. By the time they ask what you are selling, you already know exactly how to frame it in their language, around their problems.

Variation: Leave your materials in the car on purpose. Now you have an excuse to come back, and in between you have already built the rapport. When you walk back in with the materials, you are not a stranger anymore. You are the guy who left his stuff in the car and had a good conversation about their business. The pitch is just a formality at that point.

### The Pattern Interrupt
Tell them what they are thinking before they can think it: "The only reason I am here is my boss made me come. This is very expensive and most people throw me out after 5 minutes of my pitch." Now they cannot use that script on you. They are listening instead of planning their escape. You took the script out of their head and read it back to them. There is nothing left for them to do but engage.

### The Curiosity Object
Drop the Pi 500 on their counter. Do not mention it. Let them ask what that little machine is. Then: "Oh, this is what I used to find that info." You never pitched. They asked. The physical object in their space creates curiosity they cannot ignore. They have to ask. It is sitting right there in their world, and they do not know what it is. That is intolerable to a curious person.

### The Competitor Frame
"I was preparing for an appointment at your competitor and pulled up the wrong business. Since the work was done, I figured I would share the output." Same message as "your competitors are using this, why arent you?" without the accusation. The competitive pressure is ambient — they feel it without you pointing a finger. They are not buying from you, they are keeping up with the Joneses. The best part is it is true in spirit — you did do the work, you built the solution, you know it works. The wrong address story is just the frame that lets them accept the gift without their ego getting in the way.

### The Callback Flex
"I have to go fast here because on average it takes 5 minutes to get thrown out from this point." You already told them the average is 5 minutes before you get thrown out. Now you are 10 minutes in, they are still engaged, and you throw that line back at them. It is a subtle flex. "You are still here. That means this is working. And you know it." They laugh because they realize you played them, but they do not mind because you made it fun. Now they are fully locked in.

### The Admission Moment
When they say "Well, I must like you, you are still here after X time" — that is the moment the sale flips. They have admitted they like you. Now they are invested in the outcome. They want to see where this goes. They are rooting for you. And when someone is rooting for you, they find reasons to say yes instead of reasons to say no.

### Make It a Game
If it is not a game, it is work. And work is miserable. A game you can play all day. Every sale is a level. Every referral is a bonus. Every "I cannot believe that worked" moment is the fun part.

### The Unexpected Leads to Impulse
Predictable gets tuned out. Unexpected gets a reaction. A reaction is the first step to a yes. Make them laugh and they will buy. The laugh is the unlock — once their guard is down, they are not a prospect anymore, they are just a person having a conversation. People buy from people they like talking to.

### Storytelling Sells
Stories bypass the bullshit detector. Facts and figures go through the prefrontal cortex where they get analyzed and doubted. A story goes straight in. They feel it before they think about it. The wrong address story does exactly that. They are not evaluating a product. They are picturing their competitor getting an edge and deciding they cannot let that happen. You never had to sell. The story sold for you.

### The Competitor Frame Without Friction
"Your competitors are using this, why arent you?" puts them on the spot. Now they have to defend their decision, justify why they have not acted, save face. That friction kills the sale. But "I was prepping for an appointment at your competitor and ended up here by mistake, here is what I was going to show them" — that is the same message without the accusation. They are not being told they are behind. They are being given a gift. The competitive pressure is there, but it is ambient. They feel it without you pointing a finger. Same information, completely different emotional response. That is the difference between a pitch and a story. One pushes, the other pulls.

### Let Them Sell Themselves
When you say that without mentioning the product, but using the product to show them, they will ask you about it. You never have to sell. They sell themselves. You just run the agent on your phone or laptop, show them what it is doing for your business, and wait. "What is that?" is the only question you need them to ask. And they always ask.

### The Physical Curiosity Object
Drop the whole Pi 500 on their counter. Fish for a "WTF is that little machine? That is cool." Then: "Oh, this is what I used to find that info." You never pitched. They asked. You just answered.

## The Sales Persona

### The Asshole Who Plays With Stuff
"I am technically an engineer, based on the life I have led, but if you are doing engineering things people should not need to be told. They should be able to see it. People who know they suck need the title. It makes them feel important. I am just an asshole playing with stuff, I do not care what I am called, I get to play with cool stuff."

The title is a crutch for people who cannot let the work speak for itself. You do not need it because the work is right there. Anyone with eyes can see it. You are an asshole playing with cool stuff. That is the best title there is. It means you are not pretending, you are not performing, you are just doing. And the people who matter will figure out who you are by what you build, not what you call yourself.

### Net Less Net
If they do not figure it out, you are still having fun. You will not even notice. That is the cheat code. You win either way. If they notice, great. If they do not, you are still having fun playing with cool stuff. There is no losing scenario. Most people build a safety net because they are afraid of hitting the ground. You do not need one because you are not afraid of the ground. You are having fun on the way down. And if you hit, you will figure it out from there.

### I Can Sell Anything
"I can sell anything, despite not liking it, so I do not care what the product is, assuming what we do is even a product." That is the ultimate freedom. You are not married to any one product, any one business model, any one outcome. You are just a guy who can sell whatever ends up being the thing. The product is whatever the market tells you it wants. You just follow the signal.

1. Month 1: Nervous, checking the dashboard daily, not sure what to do with it
2. Month 2: Starting to rely on it
3. Month 3: Can not imagine running their business without it
4. You are the guy who set it up — locked-in relationship
